The problemPar 4

A drawing of your 12th tells a golfer nothing

Every club puts up the same three things, and a golfer who has never played you learns almost nothing from any of them.

×
The hole map.A green shape and a number on a flat page. It cannot show the carry, it cannot show the fall, and it cannot show what is hidden round the corner — which is the whole reason they were looking.
×
The photographs.One angle, chosen by you, in the best light of the year. Twelve of them still add up to less than one hole.
×
The drone film.Good footage — and it plays at them. They cannot stop on the 7th, cannot turn round, cannot ask how far that bunker is. The day you re-bunker a green it is out of date.

None of it answers the only question they are actually asking: what does this hole ask of me, and is it worth the drive? A 3D model answers it in about four seconds — because they are the one driving.

02
The modelPar 5

Your golf course. Not a rendering of one.

Nothing is drawn or invented. It is your property, surveyed, every hole standing where it really stands.

  • 25 cm aerial surveyThe real imagery — your own mowing lines, your own bunker sand, your own trees.
  • LiDAR ground surveyNational 50 cm laser scans where they exist. Dunes, banks and greens measured, not guessed.
  • Real coordinatesEvery tee, pin and bunker on its true position — which is what lets a phone find a golfer standing on it.
  • Checked against your cardEvery number matched to the card you already print. If it disagrees, it goes back.
  • Counted treesCanopies detected from the survey and planted at measured heights. On one estate that ran to 3,328 individual trees.
  • No app, everThe whole thing streams into a browser tab. Nothing to install, nothing for a member to update.

What it doesPar 5

Not a picture. A platform.

The model is the front door. Behind it is a working system the club actually runs on — and every part of it belongs to the club, not to a portal taking a cut.

  • RangefinderDistance to the pin from wherever the golfer is standing, with the slope-adjusted number alongside it.
  • Live GPS on the holeThe course knows where the phone is. The view follows the golfer up the fairway.
  • It keeps the cardWrite in the round, keep the history. The card lives with the course, not in a separate app.
  • Tee-time enquiriesVisitors book from inside the model. The lead lands with the club.
  • The pro shop, on the holePut an offer on the 7th. It becomes a numbered voucher the till scans and burns — shown, claimed and redeemed all counted.
  • Corporate daysThe GM builds a sponsored day — holes, branding, prizes — from the same dashboard.
  • Greenkeeper pinsStand on the green, tap once, and today's flag position is live for every member.
  • Your own numbersVisits, holes walked, time spent, enquiries raised — in your colours, behind your password.
05
PathfinderPar 3

The product that buys itself.

Golf GPS is a large industry, and it was built on other people's fairways. Every yardage those apps sell is measured off a golf course — and the course was never paid a cent for it. The middleman took the lot.

Pathfinder turns that around. The club sells the GPS passes, over its own counter, to its own members, and keeps the takings. We invoice monthly, and that is the whole arrangement.

Your coursethe data is yours
Your counteryou sell the pass
Your takingswe invoice monthly

This is the first time the course itself is on the paying side of golf technology. The golf club becomes the tech company.

Beyond the fairwayPar 4

A golf course is just a large, beautiful, complicated property.

So is everything else we are pointing this at. The engine does not know it is looking at golf.

  • Wine farms and gardensThe first is a Cape Winelands estate: the werf and its Cape Dutch buildings, the formal garden, 4,858 trees at real scale, and the mountain standing forty-four kilometres behind it. Ten wayfinding stops, so a visitor on foot always knows where they are.
  • Reserves and lodgesThe same engine pointed at the bush — terrain, camps, waterholes and drive routes, so a lodge can show a guest the land before they have booked the flight.
  • Estates and developmentsFly the whole estate, pick a home, walk through it. Availability updates for every visitor at once.
  • The Hole-in-One ChallengeOne shot, one credit, fifty metres across the water to an island green. The club sets the prize, and the club owns the machine. Built and live in two days.
