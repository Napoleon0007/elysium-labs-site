What it actually is

A golf club's course is its single greatest asset, and almost every club shows it as a flat drawing on a page.

A scorecard map tells a golfer nothing. It doesn't show the fall of the land, what the second shot actually looks like, or why the eleventh is the one people talk about afterwards.

So we rebuild the whole property in three dimensions — the real terrain, the real fairway shapes, the real trees at their measured heights, the real buildings — and put it in a browser window where anyone can fly over it, drop onto any tee, and look down the hole the way they'd see it standing there.

It loads from a link. It works on a phone. And it lives on the club's own website, under the club's own name.

Measured, not drawn

Nobody sketches these. Every model starts as survey data, and everything in it is somewhere real.

  • 25 cm aerial imageryThe ground you see is the actual photographed surface of the property, not a texture that looks like grass.
  • National laser scansWhere a country publishes LiDAR, the landform is measured to half a metre. Dunes, ridges and greens sit at their true height.
  • True coordinatesEvery tee, pin and bunker sits on its real latitude and longitude — which is what lets a phone know where you're standing.
  • Counted treesCanopies are detected from the survey and planted at measured heights. On one estate that ran to 3,328 individual trees.
  • Checked against the cardHole lengths are reconciled against the club's own scorecard before anyone calls a model finished.
  • Runs on a phoneThe whole thing streams into a browser tab. No app store, no install, nothing for a member to update.

Not a picture. A platform.

The model is the front door. Behind it is a working system the club actually runs on.

  • Rangefinder

    Distance to the pin from wherever the golfer is standing, with the slope-adjusted number alongside it.

  • Live GPS on the hole

    The course knows where the phone is. The view follows the golfer up the fairway.

  • Scorecard

    Write in the round, keep the history. The card lives with the course, not in a separate app.

  • Tee-time enquiries

    Visitors book from inside the model. The lead lands with the club, not with a booking portal that takes a cut.

  • Pro-shop offers

    The club puts an offer on a hole. It becomes a numbered voucher the till scans and burns. Shown, claimed and redeemed are all counted.

  • Corporate days

    The general manager builds a sponsored day — holes, branding, prizes — from the same dashboard.

  • Greenkeeper pins

    Stand on the green, tap once, and today's flag position is live for every member.

  • The club's own numbers

    Visits, holes walked, time spent, enquiries raised — in the club's colours, behind the club's password.

Pathfinder

And then the golf club becomes the tech company.

Golf GPS is a large industry that was built on other people's fairways, and the courses themselves were never paid for it. Pathfinder turns that around: the club sells the GPS passes, over its own counter, to its own members, and keeps the takings.
